How It Works
Overview

This is the glucose metabolism-based reference network. Organ–organ connections are based on the partial correlation network from a healthy cohort (n=241, BMI <24.4 kg/m²) from Geist et al. (2025). For each included pair the pipeline issues one PubMed query requiring both organs, at least one metabolic keyword, and at least one crosstalk keyword to all co-occur somewhere in the document — a coarse pre-filter. After fetching, papers are filtered further: kept only if both organs' names/aliases and a crosstalk keyword appear together in the same sentence, not merely somewhere in the same title/abstract.

Query Construction

Each organ contributes its MeSH term and all text aliases searched in Title/Abstract. Both organ clauses must co-occur with at least one metabolic keyword and at least one crosstalk keyword:

(MeSH_A OR aliases_A[Title/Abstract]) AND (MeSH_B OR aliases_B[Title/Abstract]) AND (metabolism OR metabolic OR catabolism OR anabolism OR biosynthesis OR metabolite OR flux OR substrate OR nutrient OR fuel OR glucose OR "glucose uptake" OR glycolysis OR gluconeogenesis OR energy OR bioenergetics OR mitochondria OR mitochondrial OR thermogenesis OR "Krebs cycle" OR "electron transport" OR oxidation) AND (network OR interplay OR "cross-talk" OR crosstalk OR connection OR axis OR "inter-organ" OR interorgan OR "cross-organ" OR communication OR bidirectional OR "feedback loop" OR signaling OR regulation OR mediates OR modulates OR interaction OR link OR coupling OR "cross-regulation" OR pathway)

Organ and crosstalk terms are matched as whole words, not substrings — "renal" no longer matches inside "adrenal", so a paper about the adrenal gland can't be mistaken for kidney evidence. A match is also discarded if it's negated — "non-renal", "nonrenal", "non renal", and "not renal" don't count as a positive mention of that organ or crosstalk term.

Same-Sentence Filter

The query above is a cheap pre-filter — it only guarantees both organs and a crosstalk keyword appear somewhere in the document. After fetching, a paper is kept only if there's a single sentence in its title/abstract containing organ A's name/alias, organ B's name/alias, and a crosstalk keyword all together. A paper discussing organ A in one paragraph and organ B in a completely separate one no longer counts as evidence for that pair, even though it passed the PubMed query.

Key Player Extraction

All abstracts are scanned against three curated vocabulary lists:

  • Hormones — insulin, glucagon, cortisol, leptin, GLP-1, IGF-1, FGF21, irisin, …
  • Metabolites — glucose, lactate, fatty acids, bile acids, amino acids, ATP, …
  • Proteins — GLUT1–5, AMPK, mTOR, PPARs, PGC-1α, CPT1, LPL, …

Key players are ranked by mention count and shown for each edge.

LLM Classification

For each organ pair an LLM (llama3.2, via Ollama) reads up to 100 paper abstracts and assigns 1 to 3 connection types from the categories below, ranked from most to least feasible — a pair only gets a second or third type if it is independently well-supported by the papers.

To reduce single-sample noise, the classification is run 3 times independently per pair; only types that appear in at least 2 of the 3 runs are kept (majority vote). If the 3 runs disagree on everything, the single most-voted type is kept, so every pair always ends up with at least one type.

Connection Type Categories
Direct glucose transferOne organ releases glucose into circulation that the other directly takes up as primary fuel.
Hormonal glucose controlOne organ secretes a hormone (insulin, glucagon, GLP-1, GIP) that directly regulates glucose handling in the other.
Other Hormonal influenceOne organ secretes a hormone that influences the metabolism of function of another organ while not directly affecting glucose handling.
Nerve connectionThe interaction is via an actual physical nerve connection (e.g. vagus nerve in the gut-brain-axis).
Other physical connectionNon nerve but still physical connection Could e.g. be mechanical influence of one organ on the other
Ketone body exchangeKetone bodies (beta-hydroxybutyrate, acetoacetate) produced by hepatic ketogenesis are exported and oxidized as fuel by the other organ, especially during fasting or low-carb states.
Mineral/electrolyte-coupled metabolic regulationOne organ regulates a mineral or electrolyte (calcium, phosphate, iron) whose circulating level feeds back on metabolic enzyme activity or energy handling in the other.
Adipokine/myokine signalingOne organ secretes an adipokine or myokine (leptin, adiponectin, irisin, FGF21) that acts on metabolic pathways in the other, beyond core glucose-regulating hormones.
Inflammatory/cytokine crosstalkCytokines released by one organ drive inflammation that alters metabolism or function in the other.
Metabolite crosstalkMetabolites produced by one organact directly on the other organ to influence its metabolism.
Circadian/clock synchronizationThe molecular clock in one organ entrains or is entrained by the other's clock, and misalignment between them is linked to disrupted metabolic rhythms.

All assigned types are shown as coloured badges on each edge in the network.

Edge Descriptions

Each organ–organ connection receives a short, cited scientific summary generated by llama3.2 in a two-step process:

  1. Sample — up to 25 papers are randomly sampled from the full PubMed result set for that pair (if fewer papers exist, all are used).
  2. Select — the LLM reads the 25 paper titles and abstracts and identifies the 5 most relevant ones for the specific metabolic or hormonal interaction between the two organs.
  3. Summarise — the LLM writes a 3–4 sentence cited summary based solely on those 5 papers. Citations in the text correspond to paper numbers [1]–[5] shown in the edge panel.

This ensures the description always cites a curated, relevant subset rather than generic highly-cited papers that may not address the specific connection.

Organ Descriptions

Each organ node receives a 5-sentence metabolic overview generated by llama3.2:

  1. Search — PubMed is queried for the organ combined with metabolic keywords, retrieving up to 50 papers from the last 5 years.
  2. Summarise — the LLM writes exactly 5 sentences covering the organ's primary metabolic substrates, energy production pathways, and key hormonal regulation signals.

Organ descriptions appear in the node panel when you click on an organ in the network.
